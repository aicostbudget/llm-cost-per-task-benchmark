is skipped because neither model takes images. Costs are at DeepSeek's peak list prices from the pricing
dataset at commit `3ccfe4e` / `637a530` (same DeepSeek prices in both); the runs happened off-peak, where

## What the numbers say

- On these tasks the cheaper model is not less reliable: success rates are the same within one item, so the
  cost per success is decided by price and by how many tokens each model spends.
- V4 Pro spends far more output tokens, almost all of them reasoning: on the hard tier its mean output per
  attempt was 1.3–4.3× flash's (4,294 vs 1,003 tokens on `regex_from_examples_hard`). That is why the gap
  grows from 4.9× on the standard tier to 9.2× on the hard tier, beyond the 3.3× output price ratio.
- Three V4 Pro attempts (two hard regex items, one bug fix) hit the 16,000-token output limit while still
  reasoning and returned nothing; each passed on the retry. Retries are part of the cost per success.
- `long_doc_qa` sends a ~30K-token handbook; 98–99% of its input (97.8% for V4 Pro on the standard tier) was served from DeepSeek's cache, which is
  why it costs about the same as the short-prompt tasks.

## Failures and fixes

Of the 9 first-attempt failures across both models and tiers, three were model errors (all flash: a bug
fix, a record field, a regex), three were V4 Pro output-limit cut-offs, and three were ambiguous questions
in the benchmark:

- `text_to_sql-022`, `text_to_sql_hard-008`: the databases contain two people with the same name, and the
  questions did not say whether to list a shared name once or once per person. Both questions now say.
- `tool_calling_hard-035`: "this coming Thursday" asked on a Thursday. The request now adds "(not today)".

These items were reworded after the runs; the expected answers did not change.
